COURSE: LIT 320 - Studies in Contemporary Fiction
DATE: May 21, 2026
TOPIC: Postmodernism, Metafiction, & Pynchon/Nabokov

- General vibe of Postmodern Literature: Rejects the modernist search for ultimate meaning in a fractured world. Instead, PM celebrates the chaos, plays with fragmentation, and leans heavily into irony and paranoia. Everything is a construct.

- Key Concepts:
  * Metafiction: Fiction that self-consciously addresses the devices of fiction. It reminds the reader that they are reading a book. (e.g., Pale Fire - a 999-line poem with a massive, unhinged commentary section by an unreliable narrator who might be hijacking the text).
  * Historiographic Metafiction: Blurring the lines between historical fact and intense fiction.
  * Pynchon & Paranoia: The Crying of Lot 49. The absolute obsession with finding patterns (apophenia). Is there a massive centuries-old underground postal conspiracy (The Trystero), or is Oedipa Maas just losing her mind? The text intentionally denies closure. The tension IS the point.

- High vs. Low Culture: PM completely breaks down the barrier between "high art" (classical lit, academic theory) and "pop culture" (comic books, TV jingles, detective tropes). 

- Key quote/takeaway from lecture: Modernism mourns the loss of order; Postmodernism throws a party in the ruins.
